# Cover art direction: transfer the approved look to a new topic

Use this for every new or repaired labeled mind-map cover. It supplements content, dimensions, and connector checks; passing those checks alone does not establish a good-looking page. The owner rejected the 2026-10-15 Window drafts after a structural pass because their boxy repeated pictures, generic central window, stiff ink, and fixed-column layout looked weaker than the approved Dog, Key, and Cup pages. The owner later selected `../assets/approved-window/cover.png` as the correct Window image; use that exact file as a positive **home-series** reference and do not use the earlier drafts.

## Reference roles and first look

Open the actual `key-reference.png`, `cup-reference.png`, `approved-cat-cover.png`, and approved Dog `cover.png` files before designing. Key and Cup establish the hand-drawn material, playful topic identity, and mix of close-ups, actions, and settings. Cat and Dog establish the complete-page balance of subject, numbered headings, small pictures, coral curves, and whitespace. Use the approved Carrot cover **only** to calibrate the compact left-subject size. Pass the applicable reference images as image files to an image generator when it supports them. Never copy Hello Kitty, the reference subjects, or their facts into an unrelated topic.

For home-object topics, open the approved Window cover as an additional full-page anchor: its opened sash gives the subject a useful pose; enlarged part views, actions, buildings and room scenes vary the right-side shapes; and its soft wood, curtain, flower and paper treatment feels like one illustration family. Translate those design relationships to the new object without copying Window-specific decorations into every topic. When a candidate fails, the next attempt must visibly change the failed composition or illustration vocabulary rather than merely adjust colors or spacing.

Apply the explicit Door/Window lessons and pre-generation brief in [home-series.md](home-series.md). In particular, inspect subject scale, heading-to-picture relationships, study variety and home softness separately; a correct connector network cannot compensate for failures in these areas. Do not force a subject to a size percentage when its actual silhouette and page balance fail the comparison.

Write a short visual plan beside the manifest before generating art:

1. **Subject identity:** choose one recognizable design for the main topic and a small set of topic-specific motifs that can recur where meaningful. For an inanimate object, character comes from its silhouette, material, angle, wear, soft details, and setting; do not add a face unless requested. The subject must be identifiable at 350 px page width without relying on its title.
2. **Picture vocabulary:** decide which groups need a whole object, enlarged physical part, action/sequence, location, or small scene. Each picture must teach its keyword, but adjacent groups should not all reuse the same full object or the same rectangular crop. A cozy room or building is useful only where it explains the label; it must not become the default tile for every group.
3. **Page rhythm:** sketch the subject and branch destinations as one page before typesetting. Let groups vary in picture count, width, scale, and silhouette according to content. Place each heading at its branch tip with its own pictures immediately beside or below it. Keep labels directly under the relevant pictures. Do not distribute every vignette into identical columns or force equal-height rows simply because a rendering script makes that easy.
4. **Shared rendering:** keep the same ink weight, slight line irregularity, watercolor/pencil grain, paper texture, palette restraint, light direction, and edge softness across the main subject and all mini studies. If isolated image-generation batches drift, regenerate or recolor the mismatched art before composing the final page. Avoid heavy, uniformly dark outlines and saturated wood blocks that turn the page into clip art.

## Complete-page review and rejection rule

Render one **complete labeled cover** with exact manifest text and all connector lines. At the same 300–400 px display width, place it beside Key, Cup, selected Cat, and approved Dog. Also inspect it beside Carrot for main-subject scale. Judge the whole pages, not individual art crops. Record the result and the specific revision in the topic's `prompt-notes.md`.

The cover passes only when all four questions have a clear **yes**:

- **Identity:** Is the left subject immediately recognizable and visually memorable at phone size? Do its useful motifs make the pictures feel like one topic without mechanically repeating the whole subject?
- **Hand-drawn coherence:** Do main art and mini studies visibly share the approved ink, grain, softness, color restraint, and level of detail? Does the result avoid a polished icon set or sticker-sheet look?
- **Picture variety and meaning:** Can a reader distinguish the group meanings from the pictures, with a useful mix of part close-ups, actions, objects, and settings? Are neighboring groups visibly different while still belonging to one series?
- **Page rhythm:** Do curves emerge naturally from the subject or title, and do headings lead directly into nearby pictures? At phone width, are text and pictures balanced across the page without a detached heading column, rigid icon grid, repeated rectangular tiles, dead gaps, or crowding?

If any answer is **no**, the cover fails even when dimensions, manifest, spelling, and branch count pass. Identify the largest visual cause, change the main art, vignette selection, scale, or layout as needed, then render and compare the **whole page** again. Do not repeatedly submit near-identical variants that preserve the failed composition. Do not produce the picture-only map or use the rejected cover as the next topic's style reference. Present only the best complete candidate for owner review; visual self-check does not equal owner approval.
